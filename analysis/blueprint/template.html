Navier–Stokes Blueprint

The proof's spine as load-bearing declarations, each with a one-line informal statement. Edges mean “is proved using”, after transitive reduction through the ~23,800 declarations in between. Read top to bottom: Clay statements, Theorem 1.1, then the construction down to the axisymmetric base flow.

Spine or sprawl?

The top is a clean spine. From the Clay statements down to the viscosity-1 candidate the proof is a short chain: scaling, periodization, and a classical uniqueness argument. These layers are under 5% of the code.

The one idea (paper §2–3): a swirling, axisymmetric flow concentrates on a shrinking annulus with τ = 1 − t, and its velocity grows like τ−1/2−h while its energy stays bounded because h < 1/6. The flow does not solve Navier–Stokes exactly. Oscillatory waves supply the missing stress. Then repeated corrections push the residual to zero at every order (σj = 1/5 + j/10), and the leftover residual becomes the smooth force f.

What Lean actually checks

  • The two root theorems restate DeepMind's formal-conjectures versions of Fefferman's (C) and (D) word for word. The challenge file imports only Mathlib, and the proof never imports it.
  • The witness has zero initial velocity. All the work is done by a smooth force f that is compactly supported in space and in time t > 0, which the Clay text permits.
  • Blowup is pointwise unbounded speed as t ↑ 1 (SpeedUnboundedAtOne). The proof also shows the H³ norm diverges.
  • The excluded competitors on ℝ³ are any smooth global solutions with bounded energy, the same force, and zero initial data. No support or growth condition is imposed on them. On the torus, not even an energy bound is imposed.
  • The repository prints #print axioms for both roots. I did not re-run the build for this page.

How this was built. Declarations and dependencies come from the ns-explorer extraction (data/ns-cone.json, Lean elaborated environment, proof and statement dependencies). Nodes were picked from the dominator tree of the two Clay theorems: each owns a large subproof or is a statement the rest is organized around. “Cone” is the number of source lines in the node's full transitive dependency set inside NavierStokes.
Caveats. The informal statements are my paraphrase of each declaration's Lean signature and docstring, not machine-checked text. Paper references are approximate: the Lean comments cite an earlier draft's numbering, so the mapping to the published PDF's sections was done by content.
